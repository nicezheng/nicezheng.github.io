--- layout: paper paper_id: healthcare-ai-governance title: 'Principled Convergence, Operational Fragmentation: Healthcare AI Governance in the US, EU, China, and Africa' description: The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into global healthcare workflows is advancing faster than state governance systems can adapt around the world. This Health Policy paper shifts beyond the traditional Global North versus Global South binary, providing a comparative regulatory analysis of a landscape divided between dominant technology-exporting superpowers (the United States, the European Union, and China) and technology-importing regions (the African continent). While exporting powers demonstrate principled convergence on safety, lifecycle management, and human oversight, their underlying legal architectures introduce severe operational fragmentation. For technology-importing regions, this fragmentation creates systemic vulnerabilities, exposing local populations to regulatory arbitrage and asymmetric dependencies on foreign cloud infrastructure. This operational friction has triggered an emergent narrative of Sovereign AI, wherein developing nations prioritize data sovereignty and localized compute infrastructure to guarantee algorithmic alignment with regional clinical realities. Ultimately, mitigating global health disparities requires reconciling fragmented state legal regimes with the normative ethical frameworks championed by intergovernmental and non-governmental institutions, shifting toward a unified, human-centered global regulatory science. permalink: "/papers/healthcare-ai-governance/" page_class: paper noindex: false ---
