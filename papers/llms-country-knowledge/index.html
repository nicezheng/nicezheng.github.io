--- layout: paper paper_id: llms-country-knowledge title: LLMs Know My Country Better Now, But Not Yet Good Enough description: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly serve as critical information infrastructures for global development and policy decisions. However, whether rapid advances in LLMs have reduced or reinforced geographic disparities in factual recall remains unclear. We conduct a longitudinal assessment by updating WorldBench through 2025 across 11 development indicators and 216 countries and territories, systematically evaluating an extensive suite of 33 earlier and recent LLMs across over 140K queries. We find that while recent LLMs reduce global mean error by 41.0%, geographic disparities persist, with errors remaining 1.58× as high in Sub-Saharan Africa as in North America and 1.27× as high in low-income as in high-income nations. Furthermore, open-source LLMs achieve substantial improvements yet their regional disparities remain largely unchanged, while proprietary LLMs exhibit lower errors and smaller geographic gaps. Such improvements are also uneven across development indicators, with marked gains in population and GDP but persistent stalls in critical welfare indicators such as maternal mortality and education expenditure. Enabling external retrieval serves as an effective path toward narrowing these gaps. Our findings reveal a persistent disconnect between advances in LLM factual accuracy and progress toward more geographically equitable access to socioeconomic knowledge. permalink: "/papers/llms-country-knowledge/" page_class: paper noindex: false ---
